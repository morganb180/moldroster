Mold after a flood or hurricane: what to do

Reviewed by Mold Roster staff · Last updated November 1, 2026

Quick answer: Move fast: mold can start growing within 24–48 hours of flooding. Stay safe, photograph the damage for insurance, then dry the home and remove soaked materials that can't be dried. Call a licensed pro for large areas, sewage-contaminated water or HVAC contamination.

How fast does mold grow after a flood?

FloodSmart says mold can start forming within 24–48 hours of flooding, and EPA says things that stay wet for more than two days usually get moldy.

Clean up and dry out the home fully within 24–48 hours if you can (CDC).

What should I do first?

Stay safe, then document, then dry out.

  • Turn off electricity and gas before going in, and have a qualified expert inspect before turning power back on (FloodSmart).
  • Wear gloves, goggles and an N-95 mask (FloodSmart).
  • Photograph damage before cleanup and keep samples of damaged carpet, drywall or insulation, plus receipts (FloodSmart).
  • EPA warns that vinyl floor tile may contain asbestos (don't remove it yourself; hire an asbestos professional) and that lead-based paint may be present.

What should I throw out?

Porous materials soaked by floodwater that can't be cleaned and dried quickly, such as carpet, upholstery and some drywall and insulation, may need to go.

  • CDC: remove or replace carpets and upholstery that were soaked and can't be dried right away.
  • EPA: porous materials like ceiling tiles and carpet may have to be thrown away if they become moldy.

When do I need a licensed mold pro after a flood?

For large areas, sewage or contaminated water, or a contaminated HVAC system; and in licensed states, above the size threshold.

  • EPA: call a professional when there is sewage or other contaminated water; don't run an HVAC system you suspect is contaminated.
  • Licence thresholds: 25 contiguous square feet in Texas, over 10 square feet in Florida and New York, 10 square feet or more in DC.
  • Use a licensed assessor who is independent of the remediator.

Will flood insurance pay for mold?

Standard NFIP policies don't cover mold damage, except in limited cases such as restricted access to your home after the flood.

See mold and insurance claims.

Frequently asked questions

How long after a flood does mold grow?

FloodSmart says within 24–48 hours; EPA says things that stay wet for more than two days usually get moldy.

Can I clean up flood mold myself?

Small areas, usually. EPA says call a professional for sewage or contaminated water, and for areas over about 10 square feet follow its large-scale guidance.

Should I bleach flooded walls?

CDC says bleach, at no more than 1 cup per gallon of water, works on hard surfaces. Never mix bleach with ammonia. Porous materials that stay wet may need to be removed.
