# What is a mold remediation protocol and clearance test?

Reviewed by Mold Roster staff · Last updated November 1, 2026

**Quick answer:** The protocol is the assessor's written plan for the job: where the mold is, how much, how it will be removed and what counts as done. The remediator follows it with a work plan. Afterwards the assessor checks the area against the clearance criteria. Texas and New York require this process for licensed jobs.

## What does the protocol include?

In Texas, the assessor's protocol sets out the rooms or areas, quantities, methods and clearance criteria (Occupations Code § 1958.151; TDLR).

* New York's plan adds protective equipment, occupant notice, a cost estimate and time frame, and the underlying moisture source with the type of contractor to fix it (Labor Law § 945).
* The remediator's work plan explains how it will carry out the protocol, and a copy stays on site (Texas § 1958.152; New York § 935).

## Who writes it, and why not the remediator?

A licensed assessor writes it. In Texas and New York, the same licensee can't assess and remediate the same project, so the plan and the final check are independent of the company doing the work.

In Florida, a company may not remediate a building it assessed within the previous 12 months, with a disclosed exception for Division I contractors (§ 468.8419).

## What is clearance testing?

After the work, the assessor checks the area against the clearance criteria. In Texas the job needs a passed clearance report; in New York it ends in a passed clearance report or a final status report.

* New York: the post-remediation assessment checks that the area is free of visible mold and that the work followed the plan, and notes whether the underlying cause was fixed (§ 947).
* Texas: TDLR says no national or state 'safe' level exists, so clearance is measured against the protocol's criteria.

## What do I get at the end?

A clearance report, and in Texas a Certificate of Mold Damage Remediation within 10 days, signed by the remediator and assessor and stating whether the underlying cause was corrected.

Keep it: Texas sellers must hand buyers certificates from the previous 5 years. See [mold disclosure](https://moldroster.com/mold-disclosure-real-estate/).

## What should I ask before signing?

Ask for the protocol, the work plan, the clearance criteria and who pays for re-cleaning if clearance fails.

* Ask both companies for their licence numbers in writing.
* Make sure the moisture repair is in someone's scope.
* Compare written quotes against the same protocol.

See [how to hire a mold pro](https://moldroster.com/hiring-a-mold-pro/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who writes a mold remediation protocol?**

A licensed mold assessor. In Texas and New York it can't be the same licensee that does the remediation.

**What happens if a mold clearance test fails?**

In New York, the post-remediation assessment ends in either a passed clearance report or a final status report. Ask up front who pays for re-cleaning and retesting.

**What is a Certificate of Mold Damage Remediation?**

A Texas certificate issued within 10 days of a passed clearance, signed by the remediator and assessor, stating whether the underlying cause was corrected.
