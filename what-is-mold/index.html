What is mold, and when should you call a pro?

Reviewed by Mold Roster staff · Last updated November 1, 2026

Quick answer: Mold is a fungus that grows from tiny spores wherever there is moisture. There is always some mold around, indoors and out. CDC says damp, moldy places can cause a stuffy nose, coughing, wheezing or itchy eyes in some people and more severe reactions in people with asthma or mold allergies. Fix the moisture and clean up the mold; you don't need to know what type it is.

What is mold?

Molds are fungi. They reproduce with spores that float through the air, and they grow when spores land on damp material (EPA).

  • You can't get rid of every mold spore indoors; the way to control mold is to control moisture (EPA).
  • The most common indoor molds are Cladosporium, Penicillium and Aspergillus (CDC).
  • Mold grows on paper, cardboard, ceiling tiles, wood, drywall, insulation, carpet and fabric when they stay wet (CDC).

Can mold make you sick?

It can. CDC says damp and moldy environments may cause a range of effects, or none: stuffy nose, sore throat, coughing or wheezing, burning eyes or skin rash. People with asthma or mold allergies may react severely.

  • EPA says molds produce allergens and irritants, and can trigger asthma attacks in people with asthma.
  • People with weakened immune systems or chronic lung disease can get lung infections from mold (CDC).
  • If you have symptoms, see a doctor. This page isn't medical advice.

Is black mold more dangerous?

Not in a way testing can show. CDC says Stachybotrys chartarum, a greenish-black mold, should be treated like any other mold, and no test proves a link between it and particular symptoms.

  • NIOSH: some molds can produce toxins, but the molds themselves aren't toxic or poisonous, and color doesn't tell you how dangerous a mold is.
  • Whatever the type, remove it and fix the water problem (CDC).

How do I stop mold growing?

Keep humidity low, fix leaks and dry wet materials fast.

  • CDC: keep indoor humidity no higher than 50%. EPA gives an ideal range of 30–60%.
  • Dry water-damaged materials within 24–48 hours (EPA; CDC).
  • Use exhaust fans that vent outside in kitchens and bathrooms (CDC).
  • Don't paint or caulk over mold (EPA).

When should I call a licensed mold pro?

When the mold covers more than about 10 square feet, is in the HVAC system, follows sewage or contaminated water, or keeps coming back.

  • EPA: for areas larger than about 10 square feet, follow its guidance for large-scale remediation; with sewage or contaminated water, call a professional; don't run an HVAC system you suspect is contaminated.
  • Licensing thresholds: 25 contiguous square feet in Texas, over 10 square feet in Florida and New York, 10 square feet or more in DC. See licensing by state.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to know what type of mold I have?

No. CDC says you don't need to know the type; remove it and fix the moisture problem.

Can I clean small amounts of mold myself?

Usually. EPA says you can generally handle an area under about 10 square feet yourself, following its cleanup guidance. CDC says never mix bleach with ammonia.

Does bleach kill mold?

CDC says hard surfaces can be cleaned with household products, soap and water, or no more than 1 cup of bleach per gallon of water. Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners.
