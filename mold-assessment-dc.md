# Mold assessment and remediation in Washington, DC

Reviewed by Mold Roster staff · Last updated November 1, 2026

**Quick answer:** In Washington, DC, residential mold assessment and remediation businesses need a DOEE licence, and mold of 10 square feet or more must be handled or supervised by a DOEE-licensed professional. Landlords must inspect within 7 days of a tenant's notice and remediate within 30 days.

## Who needs a DC mold licence?

A business that does residential indoor mold assessment or remediation needs a DOEE licence, and DOEE keeps a public list (D.C. Code § 8-241.03).

* DOEE: mold of 10 square feet or more must be assessed and remediated, or supervised, by a DOEE-licensed professional.
* Smaller areas can be handled by non-licensed people following DOEE's guidelines.

## What must a DC landlord do about mold?

After written or electronic notice from a tenant, the owner must inspect within 7 days and remediate within 30 days, using a licensed professional where required (D.C. Code § 8-241.04).

* Landlords must tell prospective tenants about known indoor mold contamination from the previous 3 years, unless a licensed professional remediated it (§ 42-3502.22).
* DOEE suggests keeping indoor humidity below 50%.

## Who handles DC mold complaints now?

Since June 12, 2026, DOEE no longer does residential mold complaint inspections. The Department of Buildings enforces the property maintenance code (12G DCMR).

The Office of the Tenant Advocate is at (202) 719-6560 (DOEE tenant page).

## Frequently asked questions

**How fast must a DC landlord fix mold?**

Inspect within 7 days of the tenant's written or electronic notice and remediate within 30 days (D.C. Code § 8-241.04).

**Does DOEE still inspect mold complaints in DC rentals?**

No. As of June 12, 2026, residential mold complaints go to the Department of Buildings.

**When does DC require a licensed mold pro?**

For 10 square feet or more of indoor mold, per DOEE.
