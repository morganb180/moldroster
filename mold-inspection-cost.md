# How much does a mold inspection cost?

Reviewed by Mold Roster staff · Last updated November 1, 2026

**Quick answer:** HomeAdvisor's national figures put a mold inspection at $303–$1,075, with an average of about $689. Lab samples are usually extra: about $200–$300 for a swab sample and $250–$350 for an air sample. These are national figures, not local quotes.

## What does a mold inspection cost?

HomeAdvisor reports $303–$1,075 for a mold inspection, about $689 on average, with an overall range of $150–$2,992.

* Homes under 4,000 square feet: about $300–$400 (HomeAdvisor).
* Larger homes: about $700–$1,000 (HomeAdvisor).
* On-site testing: about $250–$500 (HomeAdvisor).
* A standard inspection takes about 2 hours and can take up to 6 (HomeAdvisor).

Source: [HomeAdvisor mold inspection cost guide](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/environmental-safety/test-or-remove-mold-and-toxic-material/). For removal prices see the [remediation cost guide and estimate tool](https://moldroster.com/mold-remediation-cost/).

## How much do mold lab tests cost?

HomeAdvisor lists swab samples at about $200–$300, air samples at about $250–$350, carpet samples at about $50 and HVAC samples at about $50–$75.

| Sample type | Typical price (HomeAdvisor) |
| Swab (surface) | $200–$300 |
| Air | $250–$350 |
| Carpet | about $50 |
| HVAC | $50–$75 |

## Do I need to pay for mold testing?

Often not. EPA says that if you can see mold, sampling usually isn't needed, and there are no EPA or federal limits to compare a result against.

* EPA: when mold growth is visible, sampling is usually unnecessary ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-testing-or-sampling)).
* CDC does not recommend mold testing, and there are no set standards for an acceptable amount of mold in a home ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/mold-health/about/index.html)).
* Texas: TDLR's consumer sheet says sampling is not necessary in many cases, and there is no national or state 'safe' level ([TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/mld/pdf/cmis.pdf)).
* Testing can still help to find hidden mold when you smell it but can't see it, or to check that a cleanup worked.

See [mold testing vs inspection](https://moldroster.com/mold-testing-vs-inspection/).

## What does the clearance check after remediation cost?

No source we use publishes a separate price for it. Budget for a second assessment visit and ask for the price in writing up front.

* In Texas, a licensed assessor does a post-remediation assessment and the job needs a passed clearance report ([TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/mld/pdf/cmis.pdf)).
* In New York, the post-remediation assessment ends in a passed clearance report or a final status report (Labor Law § 947, [Article 32](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/A32)).
* Our [estimate tool](https://moldroster.com/mold-remediation-cost/) can add a clearance visit at the same price range as an inspection (an assumption, not a quoted price).

## Who can do a mold inspection?

Mold assessment is licensed work in Texas, Florida, New York and Washington, DC, with exemptions that vary by state. Hire a licensed assessor who is independent of the remediation company.

* Texas: TDLR-licensed mold assessment consultant or company.
* Florida: DBPR-licensed mold assessor.
* New York: NYS DOL-licensed mold assessment contractor.
* Washington, DC: DOEE-licensed mold assessor.

See the [licensing rules by state](https://moldroster.com/mold-licensing-by-state/), or [get free quotes](https://moldroster.com/quotes/) from licensed pros in Texas, New York or DC.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a mold inspection worth it?**

It can be when you smell mold but can't find it, after a leak or flood, before buying a home, or to confirm a cleanup worked. If mold is visible, EPA says sampling is usually unnecessary; fix the moisture and remove the mold.

**Does a home inspection include mold?**

Not necessarily. In New York, the NYS Department of Labor says home inspectors need a Mold Assessor licence if their reports assess mold.

**Why do mold test results come without a 'safe' number?**

There are no EPA or federal limits for mold in indoor air, and CDC says there are no set standards for an acceptable quantity of mold in a home.
